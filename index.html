    Guides

    Everything you need to get set up and get the most out of Pad Practice.

    Map your pads

    Teach Pad Practice which of your real pads is the kick, the snare, and the rest. It takes about a minute, and you only have to do it once.

    How it works

    You hit a padFor example, one of the big pads on your SPD.
    It sends a noteEvery pad sends its own MIDI note number, like Note 38.
    The app finds its squareMapping links Note 38 to square 5, the Snare.
    Your hit is gradedRight sound at the right moment counts as on time.

    When do you need it?

    Timing only and rudiments

    Not needed. Any pad counts, so you can start right away.

    Beats and your own beats

    Needed, so the app knows which sound you played. Unmapped pads still count, and the app learns them as you play.

    Left and right hand

    For rudiments, set which real pads count as your right and left hand in Setup.

    Three ways to map

    1. Learn all notes (fastest)

      Under the pads in Practice, press Learn all notes. The app lights up square 1. Hit the real pad you want for it, and it moves on to square 2, then 3, and so on to 9. The three slim squares are the top row.

      Tap any square to jump to it, or press Stop learning to finish early.

    2. Change one square

      Press Edit layout and tap a square. Pick its sound and light color, then press Learn from pad and hit the real pad you want. Clear removes the link again.

    3. Let the app learn while you play

      Just start a beat. When a pad isn’t mapped yet, the app matches your hit to the note the beat expects at that moment. After a few consistent hits, it links the pad for you and shows “Pad learned”.

    Check your mapping

    Hit any pad on your SPDConnect your pad first, under Setup or with the button in the top corner.
    Waiting for a hit…

    Each square also shows its note number under the name, and lights up when you hit its pad.

    Hand pads and saved layouts

    Right and left hand

    In Setup, under Hand pads and sounds, press Set right-hand pad or Set left-hand pad and hit that pad. On screen, tap a pad’s left or right edge instead.

    Save your mapping

    Your mapping is kept automatically. To keep several setups, give each one a name in Setup, under Pad layouts. A layout saves sounds, notes, and light colors.

    Map again when things change

    If you change the note numbers on your pad, or switch to a pad kit that uses other notes, map again or switch to a saved layout.

    Troubleshooting

    A square still says “Not mapped”

    Press Edit layout, tap that square, press Learn from pad, and hit the real pad. Or run Learn all notes again.

    Two real pads light up the same square

    Both pads send the same note number. Give one of them a different note in your pad’s MIDI settings, then map it again.

    My hits show up as “Extra”

    That pad is linked to a sound that isn’t in this beat at that moment. Check the square’s name, and remap it if it’s the wrong sound.

    The mapping stopped matching

    Your pad may be sending other notes now, for example after switching kits on the pad. Run Learn all notes again, and save a layout for each pad kit you use.

    Nothing happens when I hit a pad

    Check that the light on the Pads connected button blinks when you hit. If it doesn’t, your pad isn’t sending MIDI to the app yet. The connection guide in Setup walks you through it.

    Connect your drum pad

    Everything you need to get your pad talking to Pad Practice, step by step. It takes about two minutes, and you usually don’t need to install anything.

    What you need

    Your pad

    A Roland SPD-SX PRO, or any pad or e-drum module that sends MIDI over USB.

    A USB data cable

    USB-B on the pad side. The other end is USB-A or USB-C, whichever your computer has. Charge-only cables won’t work.

    Chrome or Edge

    On Windows, Mac, Linux, or a Chromebook. On Android, use Chrome with a USB OTG adapter.

    Usually no driver

    With Driver Mode set to GENERIC, the driver built into your computer handles MIDI.

    Will it work on my device?

    Computer

    Windows, Mac, Linux, or Chromebook with Chrome or Edge.

    Android phone or tablet

    Chrome, plus a USB OTG adapter for the cable.

    iPhone and iPad

    No browser on iPhone or iPad can use MIDI devices yet.

    Safari on Mac

    Safari can’t use MIDI devices. Use Chrome or Edge on your Mac.

    Step by step

    1. Set Driver Mode to GENERIC

      On the SPD-SX PRO, press MENU, open the SYSTEM tab, and choose USB AUDIO. Set Driver Mode to GENERIC with the VALUE knob. Then unplug the USB cable and plug it back in, because the new mode only starts after reconnecting.

      Want to record USB audio as well? Choose VENDOR instead, and install Roland’s driver first (see Software and drivers below).

    2. Plug in the cable

      Plug the USB-B end into the COMPUTER port on the back of the pad, not the USB MEMORY port. Plug the other end straight into your computer rather than into a hub.

    3. Switch on the pad, then open Pad Practice

      Turn the pad on first. Then open or reload Pad Practice in Chrome or Edge, as its own website rather than inside another page.

    4. Press Connect pads and choose Allow

      Your browser asks whether Pad Practice may use your MIDI devices. Choose Allow. If you blocked it before, click the lock icon next to the web address, allow MIDI devices, and reload the page.

    Troubleshooting

    It says “Can’t connect”

    Open Pad Practice as its own website, not inside another page or app. Use Chrome or Edge. If you blocked MIDI earlier, click the lock icon next to the web address, allow MIDI devices, and reload.

    My pad isn’t in the device list

    Check that Driver Mode is GENERIC, or VENDOR with Roland’s driver installed, and that you replugged the cable after changing it. Use a data cable in the COMPUTER port, plug it straight into the computer, and switch the pad on before opening the page. Still nothing? Try another USB port.

    Another program may be using the pad

    On Windows, usually only one program can use a MIDI device at a time. Close music software such as a DAW, then press Connect pads again.

    It’s connected, but the pads don’t do anything

    Look at the test above. If hits show up there, go to Practice and press Learn all notes so the app knows which pad is which. If nothing shows up, unplug the cable, plug it back in, and press Connect pads.

    My hits always seem late

    Go to Setup and press Calibrate automatically. Tap along with the clicks on your real pads, and the app corrects the delay for you.

    Connection

    Plug your pad in over USB, then connect. Use Chrome or Edge.

    If every hit reads late even when it feels right, slide this up until steady playing lands near zero, or let the app measure it for you.

    Tempo sync with your pad

    Pad Practice can send its tempo to your pad as MIDI clock, so the pad’s own click and loops follow along while you practice.

    On the SPD-SX PRO, set Sync Mode to AUTO in its MIDI settings (SYSTEM MIDI). It then follows the tempo whenever Pad Practice is playing. Gap click only silences the app’s own click.

      Welcome to Pad Practice

      A free practice studio for electronic drum pads. Play along with the click and see exactly how close every hit lands.

      1. Connect your padPlug it into a computer over USB, open this page in Chrome or Edge, and press Connect pads. No pad nearby? Tap the pads on screen or press F and J.
      2. Teach the app your padsPress Learn all notes under the pads, then hit each real pad in turn so the app knows which is which.
      3. Pick a drill and press StartChoose a rudiment or beat under Drill. After a one-bar count-in, the lit pads show what to hit. Green is on time, blue is early, and orange is late.
      4. Level upEarn XP for on-time notes and finished sessions. New drills and beats unlock as you level up, and trophies mark your milestones.
      5. Make it yoursBuild and share your own beats in Beat maker, switch drum kits under the pads, and fine-tune everything in Setup.

      On screen, tap the middle of a pad for a center hit, or its left or right edge for a rim shot with that stick.

      Practice

      • See every hit on the timing meter, early or late in milliseconds, and how steady your playing is.
      • Follow the lit pads and the beat grid, or press Listen first to hear a beat before you play it.
      • Pick from 7 drum kits, from metal to jazz, with the Kit menu under the pads.
      • Play accents loud and ghost notes soft. With real pads, the app checks how hard you hit.
      • Turn on auto speed-up in Practice options to raise the tempo after clean bars.
      • Use gap click to drop the click for a bar or two and test your own sense of time.
      • After each session, your report shows where you rush or drag and what to practice next, and you can replay the session.
      • Play along with backing tracks, practice whole songs, odd time signatures, flams, drags, and polyrhythms.
      • Use the speed builder to find your top clean tempo, and follow the 30-day course under Play → Lessons.

      Beat maker

      • Build your own beats with notes, accents, and ghost notes, export them as MIDI for your DAW, and browse the beat library.
      • Save them to My beats in the Drill menu, or share them with a code.

      Progress

      • Earn XP and level up to unlock new drills and beats, all the way to level 10.
      • Take on a new daily challenge for bonus XP, collect 18 trophies, and follow your accuracy and tempo over time on the dashboard.
      • Download a backup to keep your progress safe or move it to another device.

      Setup

      • Connect your pad and let the app measure your latency automatically.
